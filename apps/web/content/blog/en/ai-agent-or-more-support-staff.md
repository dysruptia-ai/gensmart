An AI agent makes sense when your support has a high volume of repeated questions, needs to cover hours your team cannot, and most answers can be written in advance. More staff makes sense when cases are complex, depend on judgment and the human touch, or when volume is low and steady. In most businesses, the reasonable answer is to combine both.

## Which weighs more: volume or complexity?

Both, but they point to different places. A high volume of simple questions favors the agent: it handles many conversations at once without them piling up. High complexity favors people: a delicate complaint, a negotiation or an exception needs judgment and empathy. The GenSmart agent does not close sales, negotiate or collect payment, and it does not give medical, legal or financial advice, so those cases belong to your team.

## What about hours?

If your customers write at night, on weekends and on holidays, covering those hours with people requires shifts and coordination. An agent answers at any hour with the same information. Of course, whatever needs a person is left noted for when your team is back.

## How do they compare in cost?

In qualitative terms: a person is a fixed cost, constant, paid whether there are many conversations or few, and added as volume demands more people. An agent looks more like a variable cost, tied to platform usage, and grows differently. The useful question is not which is cheaper, but what happens with each option if your volume doubles or drops by half.

## Which criteria help you decide?

- <strong>Volume:</strong> are most of your conversations questions you already know how to answer?
- <strong>Hours:</strong> do people write when your team is not around?
- <strong>Complexity:</strong> how many cases truly need judgment?
- <strong>Stability:</strong> is your demand steady or does it have strong peaks?

## What if I combine both?

It is the most common and usually the most sensible. The agent absorbs repeated questions and the hours your team does not cover, and your team focuses on what carries human value: negotiating, resolving delicate cases, closing and caring for key customers. That way, adding staff stops being the automatic answer to growth and becomes a decision about which work truly needs a person.

:::nota
Before hiring someone else, note for one week what questions your team handles and how many are repeated. That list tells you whether you need more hands or better information available.
:::
