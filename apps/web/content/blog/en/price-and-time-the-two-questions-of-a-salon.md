In beauty and wellness almost every conversation starts the same way: how much is it and when is there room. An AI agent answers with the prices and durations you loaded, asks which location and time slot suit the customer, and books the appointment or trial class. Payment, packages and memberships are handled by a person.

## Why is that first message so easy to lose?

Because it arrives with your hands full: in the middle of a session, between clients, with reception busy. Whoever writes is often looking at other places at the same time. If the answer is slow, they book where they were answered first. And reception, answering in order of arrival, cannot tell who already wants to book from who is just curious.

## What does the agent answer?

- Services, prices, duration and professionals, just as you loaded them.
- Locations, hours and addresses, separately for each location.
- Current promotions, if you included them.

If asked something that is not loaded, it does not make it up: it hands the conversation to a person.

## How does it book an appointment or a class?

It asks which service they want, which location suits them best and which day and time slot they prefer. At a gym or studio, it asks about the class or plan they are interested in. Then it offers the free times on your calendar and records the booking, with the GenSmart calendar or Google Calendar.

## How does your reception know who to write to first?

With the score from 0 to 10. The agent assigns it based on the interest the person shows and places them in your funnel as a lead or an opportunity. When the score is high, your team gets a notification and writes first to whoever already said which service they want and for when.

## What remains a person's job?

Payment, package sales and memberships. If the system where you manage them has an API, such as AgendaPro or Mindbody, we look in the demo at how to query them from the agent. Changing or canceling an appointment is not something it does today either: it passes the conversation to your team. And it does not write first or send reminders.

## What changes between one location and another?

Almost everything: the hours, the address, sometimes the professionals and even the prices. That is why it pays to load each location separately. The agent answers with the information of the location the customer chose and offers the times of that calendar, so nobody gets one branch's hours when they wanted another. For chains and franchises, that consistency is what avoids the confusion that ends with a customer waiting at the wrong door. If a promotion or a price changes, you fix it in one place and every answer is updated.

:::nota
Write each service with its price, its duration and one line on what it includes. The "does it include this?" questions are among the most frequent and the ones most worth having settled.
:::
