A pipeline is the ordered list of stages a prospect goes through, from the first message until they buy or are dropped. To be useful it needs three things: few stages, one owner per stage and a written rule for when a contact moves forward. Without that, it becomes a list nobody updates.

## How many stages should it have?

As few as possible. Each stage should describe a situation you can recognize by reading the conversation, not a hunch. "New", "Qualified", "Appointment set", "Proposal sent" and "Closed" cover many businesses. If you hesitate between two similar stages, merge them.

## Who is responsible for each stage?

A person or an agent, never "the team" in general. When a stage belongs to everyone, it belongs to no one. A typical split: the GenSmart agent handles new and qualified contacts; sales takes over from the appointment; management reviews closings and dropped contacts.

## When does a contact move to the next stage?

When a verifiable fact happens, not when someone feels it should. Write the entry condition for each stage:

- <strong>Qualified:</strong> gave the key detail (what for and by when) and the score is above the threshold you set.
- <strong>Appointment set:</strong> there is an appointment on the calendar.
- <strong>Proposal sent:</strong> a person sent the quote.
- <strong>Dropped:</strong> said no or asked not to be contacted again.

GenSmart scores the contact from 0 to 10 based on the conversation and places them in the CRM pipeline. When the score is high, it alerts you so a person steps in on time.

## What details should each contact have in the pipeline?

Just enough for anyone to pick up the conversation without asking again. A useful record carries the name, the channel they wrote from, what they need, by when, the score and the current stage. Everything else is checked in the full conversation, which stays saved. If you ask for ten manual fields on each record, your team will stop filling them in and the pipeline will lose value.

## What to do with dropped contacts?

Keep them with the reason. Knowing whether they left over price, timing or because it was not their moment tells you what to adjust in your offer or your answers. A dropped contact without a reason is a lost learning opportunity, and one who asked not to receive more messages must stay marked that way for good.

:::nota
Once a week, check which contacts have been in the same stage the longest. That is the cheapest pending work to recover.
:::

## How do you keep the pipeline from getting messy?

With small, visible rules: one name per stage, one criterion per stage and a regular review. Start with the GenSmart free trial, let the agent place the first conversations and adjust the stages based on what you see.
