After the click on an ad, what should happen is simple: the person writes, gets a useful answer within seconds and knows the next step. Everything else (the creative, the targeting, the budget) depends on that first reply existing. An ad that leads to an unanswered conversation is money spent with nothing to show for it.

## Why are so many contacts lost right after the ad?

Because the click is an impulse. The person writes while the question is fresh and, if nobody answers, opens another tab and writes to a competitor. The delay does not show on the ad dashboard, but it shows in the conversations left unanswered.

## What should be ready before you turn the ads on?

- <strong>Answers to what the ad promises.</strong> If the ad mentions a promotion, the agent must know its conditions, dates and limits.
- <strong>A first message that picks up the ad.</strong> The person arrived for something specific; if the reply is generic, they feel they are starting from zero.
- <strong>A handoff rule.</strong> Which cases go to a person and who receives them at each hour.
- <strong>A clear goal.</strong> Appointment, quote, visit or purchase: the conversation must know where it is heading.

## What mistakes keep repeating with ad spend?

Three come up again and again. The first is sending people to a form when they were already ready to write. The second is promising in the ad something the reply does not confirm, like a price or a date. The third is measuring only clicks and not how many people received an answer. When the conversation is the destination of the ad, the quality of the service is part of the campaign, just like the image or the text.

:::nota
Before raising the budget, message your own ad as if you were a customer and time how long it takes for a useful reply. If it does not convince you, it will not convince anyone.
:::
