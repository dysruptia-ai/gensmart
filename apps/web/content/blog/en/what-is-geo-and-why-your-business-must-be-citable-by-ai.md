GEO (generative engine optimization) is the work of preparing your content and information so AI answer engines can understand you, trust you and cite you when someone asks something related to your business. It matters because a generated answer does not show a list of ten links: it shows an answer with a few sources.

## What changes compared with traditional search?

Before, the person searched, opened several pages and compared. Now they often ask in natural language and receive a ready-made answer. If your business does not appear as a source, it does not exist for that person at that moment, even if your site ranks well in the traditional list.

## What makes an AI cite a business?

- That the content answers the question directly and clearly, without detours.
- That the name, address, services and hours are consistent everywhere the business appears.
- That there is original, verifiable information, not generic phrases that could belong to anyone.
- That the site is well structured: clear headings, sections by question and visible contact details.
- That the content is kept up to date.

## Does GEO replace SEO?

No. It builds on it. A site that search engines cannot read will not be readable by AI either. GEO adds a layer: writing so that a piece of your content can be extracted and cited without losing its meaning.

## What can a small business do starting today?

The most profitable move is the basics done well: one page per service answering customers' real questions, a complete and current business profile, and original details others lack, such as your process, coverage areas or terms. At Dysruptia, the digital marketing agency behind GenSmart, we work on that foundation before any advanced tactic.

## What mistakes keep you from being cited?

The most common ones are simple. Pages that repeat the same empty phrase about quality and service without saying what you do. Different information on the site, the profile and social accounts. Old content with prices or terms that no longer apply. And sites where the important part sits inside images, which neither a search engine nor an AI can read well. None of them requires new technology, only order and regular review.

:::nota
Run the test: ask an AI what your customers ask you and see whether it mentions you. If it does not, you have your to-do list.
:::

Start with a single page, the one for the service you sell most, and check that it clearly says what you do, for whom, where and how to contact you. When that one works, repeat the method with the rest.
