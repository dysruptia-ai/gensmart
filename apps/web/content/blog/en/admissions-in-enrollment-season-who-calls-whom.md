In enrollment season many messages arrive in a few days and the admissions team cannot read them all with the same attention. An AI agent answers each one right away, suggests the program based on what the applicant is after and gives them a score from 0 to 10, so advisors call first the people who have already decided.

## What do applicants ask?

Almost always three things: which program suits them, how much it costs and when it starts. Then come duration, format and requirements. They are answers advisors repeat all day, and that do not change from one applicant to the next.

## How does the agent answer by program?

With the information you loaded for each one: duration, format, costs, start dates and requirements. If something changes, you update it there. If the applicant asks something that is not loaded, the agent does not make up the answer and hands the conversation to a person.

## How does it suggest the right program?

It asks what they want to study, in what format and for when, and proposes the one that fits best among those you loaded. It is a suggestion. The final decision is made by the applicant with your advisor.

## How does admissions decide who to call first?

- The agent scores from 0 to 10 based on the interest shown in the conversation.
- It places the applicant in your funnel as a lead or an opportunity.
- Admissions gets a notification when the score is high.
- If interest is high, it offers free calendar times for the call, with the GenSmart calendar or Google Calendar.

## What stays outside the agent?

Enrollment, scholarships and discounts are handled by a person. The agent does not take payment or change calls already booked either: if the applicant wants to move the time, the conversation goes to admissions. And it does not write first to someone who asked a week ago; it replies when the applicant writes, and on WhatsApp within 24 hours of their last message.

## What changes for the admissions team?

Advisors stop opening each message to find out what it is about and start the day with an organized inbox: who wrote, about which program, for when and with what score. Every channel ends up in the same place, whether WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger, your website or email. Whoever already has a call booked arrives at that call with the conversation in view, and whoever is still comparing keeps getting answers without anyone having to attend to them at that moment. The team spends its time explaining the process to people who are deciding, not repeating dates and costs.

:::nota
If you have several similar programs, write in each sheet who it is for and who it is not for. It helps the agent suggest better and avoids applicants landing in the wrong program.
:::
