Publishing an agent is easy. What is hard is regaining the trust of a customer to whom the agent said something wrong on day one. This list is not exhaustive, but it covers what fails most.

## The conversation

1. The greeting says who the agent is and what it can do. If it is a virtual assistant, it says so.
2. It answers in the customer's language and in your brand's tone, not in a manual's.
3. Answers are short. On WhatsApp nobody reads five paragraphs.
4. It asks for data one item at a time, not a full form in a single message.

## The knowledge

1. The twenty most frequent questions have a correct answer.
2. A question outside the base is recognized as such: the agent does not make things up.
3. Prices, hours and policies match what your website says today.

## The limits

1. A customer who asks for a person reaches a person. Test it for real.
2. An angry message triggers the handoff.
3. Outside hours the agent says so and offers a next step.
4. The agent does not promise what your team cannot deliver.

## The technical side

1. The connected WhatsApp number is the right one and messages arrive in the inbox.
2. Notifications reach whoever must handle the handoffs.
3. Captured data (name, phone, interest) reaches the CRM.
4. The WhatsApp templates you are going to use are approved.

## Test with people who are not you

You already know what to ask and how. Ask two or three people, preferably without technical context, to write to the agent the way they would to your business. The mistakes they find in ten minutes are usually different from those you would find in an hour.

:::nota
Start with a single channel and a limited schedule. It is easier to fix things with little traffic than with all your volume on top.
:::
