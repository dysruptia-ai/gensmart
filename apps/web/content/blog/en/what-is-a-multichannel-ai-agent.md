A multichannel AI agent is a single agent connected to several messaging channels, for example WhatsApp, Instagram and your website, that answers with the same information and leaves all conversations in one place. It matters because your customers do not pick a channel for your convenience, but for theirs.

## What problem does handling channels separately create?

When each channel lives in a different tool, someone has to check three inboxes, copy details from one to another and remember what was said where. Usually one of those inboxes goes unattended, and that is exactly where the customer who was going to buy was waiting.

## What changes with a single place?

- Answers are consistent: price and hours do not change depending on the channel.
- If the same person writes on Instagram and later on WhatsApp, whoever handles it sees what came before.
- The score and funnel stage live in a single record, not three.
- Your team works from one inbox instead of several apps.

## Does every channel behave the same?

No, and it helps to know. WhatsApp has its own Meta rules: the agent replies within the 24-hour window from the customer's last message. On Instagram people write short messages and expect speed. On the web, the person is often about to decide. The agent is the same, but the tone adapts to the channel.

## Where should you start?

Start with the channel where you receive the most messages today and add the others once the first one works well. You do not need to connect everything on day one. GenSmart connects WhatsApp through Meta's Embedded Signup flow, with no code, and the other channels are added to the same agent.

## What does it look like in your team's day to day?

In the morning, someone opens the inbox and finds the night's conversations already organized: which ones the agent resolved, which it left pending and which have a high score. It does not matter whether the message arrived through Instagram or the web, the contact record is the same. That person decides whom to write to first using a visible criterion instead of guessing.

## What mistakes are worth avoiding?

- Connecting every channel at once and not reviewing any of them properly.
- Writing a different knowledge base for each channel, instead of one that stays current.
- Forgetting that each channel has its own rules and response windows.
