In a restaurant, messages arrive exactly when nobody can answer them: Friday night, Sunday lunch. An AI agent answers with the menu and hours of each location, takes bookings and orders, and scores event and catering requests so your sales team calls first the ones worth the most.

## What does it answer about the menu and locations?

What you loaded for each location: menu, prices, hours, address and policies. The customer gets the same answer no matter which location they wrote to. With allergens and ingredients, the limit is strict: it only says what you loaded. If it is not there, it does not make it up and hands the conversation to a person.

## How does it take a booking?

It asks for the day, the time and how many people, offers the free times on your calendar and records the booking, with the GenSmart calendar or Google Calendar. Your team receives the notice. It does not change or cancel bookings today: if the customer wants to move it, it passes the conversation to your team with the summary.

## And an order?

It takes what your kitchen needs to know and repeats the summary before closing it, so the customer confirms. If your ordering system or point of sale has an API, we look in the demo at how to send it directly. If your payment gateway has an API, the agent can generate a payment link and share it in the conversation; that is also defined in the demo.

## What does it do with an event or a catering request?

1. Asks for the date, the number of people, the location and the contact details.
2. Scores from 0 to 10 and places the contact in your funnel as a lead or an opportunity.
3. Notifies your sales team when the score is high.
4. Hands over the full conversation. The proposal, the price and the close are handled by a person.

## What changes for the team at rush hour?

Nobody has to leave the kitchen or the service to answer a phone. Simple questions resolve themselves, bookings land on the calendar and orders arrive with the summary already built. What does need a person, such as a complaint or an event, waits in the inbox for whoever has a free moment, with the full conversation. And since each location answers with its own information, the customer does not receive one location's menu when they asked about another. The sales team concentrates on what is worth the most: the proposal for the event that already has a date and a head count.

:::nota
Separate in your knowledge base what changes by location (hours, menu) from what is the same everywhere (booking policies). That way you fix an hour without touching the rest.
:::
