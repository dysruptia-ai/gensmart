Each channel attracts a different kind of question. In general, WhatsApp concentrates purchase decisions and follow-ups, Instagram receives quick questions from someone who just saw a post and the website serves those who are reading and comparing. The point is not to pick one, but to answer well in each and keep the customer's story from splitting in three.

## What questions arrive on WhatsApp?

The ones that already carry intent: quotes, availability, payment methods, order status, scheduling an appointment. The person usually has the phone at hand and expects to continue the conversation during the day. Here it pays for the agent to collect the full details and leave everything ready so a person can pick up without repeating questions.

## What questions arrive on Instagram?

Short, visual questions: "price?", "do you have it in another color?", "where are you located?". It is a discovery channel, so answers should be brief and lead to a clear next step, whether that is seeing the catalog, booking or continuing the conversation elsewhere.

## What questions arrive on the website?

Those from someone reading your page who has a specific doubt before deciding: how it works, what is included, what the difference is between two options. An agent on the website answers on the spot, without forcing the person to go look for a form or a phone number.

## What does each channel look like in practice?

- <strong>WhatsApp:</strong> longer messages, complete details, follow-up throughout the day.
- <strong>Instagram:</strong> short questions, short answers and a clear step at the end.
- <strong>Website:</strong> decision doubts, explained answers, the option to book.

A practical detail about WhatsApp: the agent replies within the 24-hour window from the customer's last message and does not write first. That is why the first exchange should resolve as much as possible, and the customer should know what happens if they do not write back that same day.

## Which channel should you prioritize?

The one your customers already use. Look at where they write to you today and start there, instead of opening all of them at once. When the first one works well, adding another is easier, because the knowledge base is already written and tested. An open channel nobody serves is worse than a closed one.

:::nota
The same answer can be worded differently depending on the channel. If your knowledge base has the right data, the agent adapts the form. What must not vary is the information: the price is the same on all three.
:::
