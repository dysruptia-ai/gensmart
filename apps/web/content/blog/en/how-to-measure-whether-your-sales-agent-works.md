A sales agent is not measured by how many messages it answers, but by what changed in your business since it arrived. A dashboard with twenty indicators is of little use if you do not know which to look at. Start with four questions.

## 1. How long does it take to answer?

It is the most visible improvement and the easiest to check: compare how long your team took to answer a first message with how long it takes now. If you sell by chat, that difference often explains a good share of the leads that used to go cold.

## 2. How many conversations end in a useful piece of data?

A conversation that ends with no name, no contact and no identified interest is noise. Count how many leave at least one piece of data someone can act on: a phone number, a product, a date.

## 3. Are the leads the agent marks as hot actually hot?

Take ten high-score and ten low-score leads from the last month and check what happened with each. If the high ones closed more, the score helps you order your day. If not, adjust which signals count.

## 4. How many reached a person and why?

Handoffs tell a story. If most arrive for the same question, that question is missing from your knowledge base. If they arrive for anger, review the tone or the timing. This is the data that improves the agent most from week to week.

## How to do it without it becoming a job

- Pick a fixed day of the week and review only these four things
- Read ten random conversations, not only the ones that went wrong
- Note one improvement per week and check next week whether it worked

:::nota
Before you turn the agent on, write down where you are today on these four questions. Without a starting point, any improvement is an impression, not a fact.
:::
