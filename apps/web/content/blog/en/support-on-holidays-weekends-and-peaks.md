To cover holidays, weekends and peaks without night shifts, split the work in two: what the agent can resolve alone, such as questions with a fixed answer and first details, and what is left noted for a person the next business day. The customer gets an immediate reply and your team rests without losing a single conversation.

## Why are holidays and weekends different from night hours?

Because they last longer and are less predictable. A night is covered with a message and a morning review. A three-day holiday weekend demands more: people ask whether there are deliveries, whether they can book, whether the shop is open. If nobody defined it beforehand, the agent can only say it does not know.

## What should the agent know before a holiday?

- Which days you operate, with what hours and when the team returns.
- Whether there are deliveries or service on those days and under what conditions.
- What can be booked and from what date there is availability.
- Which cases wait until the next business day, stated clearly to the customer.

## What is left for the next business day?

Everything that needs human judgment: custom quotes, complaints, exceptions, changes to orders or appointments. The agent does not cancel or reschedule appointments, and decisions about money or terms are made by a person. What it does is leave each case summarized in the inbox, with the contact's score, so the team starts with what matters most.

## How do you handle peaks?

A peak is a holiday with more messages per hour. The preparation is the same: an updated base, clear handoff rules and an on-call person only for high-score cases, if you decide to have one. The agent serves many people in parallel, so the bottleneck stops being the first reply and becomes the human decision, which you can organize.

When the team is back, the order of work matters. Start with high-score contacts and conversations where the customer expressed urgency, and leave for last the general questions that were already resolved. That way the first thing your team handles is what cools down most with waiting.

## How do you speak to the customer when nobody on the team is in?

With honesty and with dates. Customers tolerate waiting if they know how long and why. What annoys them is the vague promise or the reply that ignores that it is Sunday. The agent can acknowledge the context, resolve what it does know and say when a person will contact them, without promising an hour you do not control.

:::nota
Write each holiday message with a specific date: "we are back Tuesday at 8 a.m." is more useful than "we will be back soon". And remember to remove it when it ends.
:::

Before the next long weekend, open your calendar, note the days without a team and load that information into GenSmart. Then test with the conversation of an imaginary customer who writes on a Sunday.
