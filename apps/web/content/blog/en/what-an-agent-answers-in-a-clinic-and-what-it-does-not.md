An AI agent in a clinic answers the logistics with the information you loaded, offers times from your calendar and books the appointment. It does not give medical advice or interpret symptoms. When the question is clinical, it hands the conversation to a person on your team.

## What does your front desk repeat every day?

Almost always the same things: which services you offer, what hours you are open, where the clinic is, how to prepare for a cleaning or an exam, how to request an appointment. They are questions with a fixed answer. While the receptionist answers them one by one, the patient standing at the desk waits.

## What does the agent do?

1. Answers services, hours, preparation and location with what you loaded.
2. Checks the availability of the calendar you connect, for example Google Calendar, and proposes free times.
3. Records the appointment and emails the patient the confirmation, with a link to save it to their calendar.
4. Hands reception the full conversation when something needs a person.

## What should it never do?

It does not diagnose, recommend treatments or comment on symptoms. If a patient writes something clinical or sensitive, the agent does not improvise: it passes it to your team with the summary. It does not cancel or move appointments today either: when the patient wants to change one, the conversation goes to reception. And it does not write first or send reminders, because it replies when the patient writes, and on WhatsApp only within 24 hours of their last message.

## What about patient data?

Each contact is saved in your GenSmart CRM with their conversation. Before deciding what information you ask for when booking, check the health data regulations that apply to you. Ask only for what is needed to give the appointment.

## How is the work split with the front desk?

While the agent handles the repetitive part, the receptionist keeps what needs presence or judgment: the patient who walks in, the one with a sensitive question, the change to an appointment, internal coordination. It is not about taking work away from anyone, but about no longer answering the same opening hours for the fifth time. The agent also answers at night and on weekends, and the appointment is booked for when you open. Reception sees each conversation in full, so whoever picks up a case knows what was said. A useful rule: if you doubt whether a question is logistical or clinical, treat it as clinical and let a person answer it.

:::nota
Make a list of the questions your team considers clinical and load it as a rule: faced with any of them, the agent does not answer and hands the conversation over.
:::
