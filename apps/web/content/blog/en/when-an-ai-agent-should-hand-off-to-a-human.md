Most problems with an AI agent do not come from what it answers badly. They come from what it keeps answering after it should have stopped. An angry customer who gets three polite replies in a row does not calm down, they leave.

That is why handing off to a person is not a plan B. It is part of the design. Before you publish the agent, decide when it hands the conversation over, to whom and with what information.

## Five situations that almost always call for a person

1. <strong>The customer asks for it.</strong> If they write "I want to talk to someone", the answer is yes, with no questions in between.
2. <strong>There is anger or a complaint.</strong> Words like scam, lawsuit or complaint, or sustained capital letters, signal that it is no longer a question, it is a relationship at risk.
3. <strong>Money is involved.</strong> Refunds, duplicate charges, off-list discounts and any exception to your policies.
4. <strong>The topic is sensitive.</strong> Health, legal matters or personal data the agent should not interpret.
5. <strong>The agent does not know.</strong> If the answer is not in its knowledge base, the right move is to say so and hand the case over, not to make something up.

## What the handoff should carry

The worst handoff is the one that makes the customer repeat everything. The person who receives the case needs to see, without digging, what the customer asked, what was answered and what is still open.

- A two or three line summary of the conversation
- The data already captured: name, product, city, whatever is relevant
- The reason for the handoff, in plain words
- The urgency: whether it can wait or someone has to answer now

:::nota
In GenSmart the handoff lands in the inbox with the conversation context and the lead score, so whoever replies does not start from zero.
:::

## The message the customer sees

When handing over, the agent should say three things: that a person is stepping in, what that person will do and how long it may take. If nobody is on shift, say that too: "Someone from the team will write to you tomorrow from 8". Promising an immediate answer that does not exist is worse than saying nothing.

## How to know if your rules work

Read the handed-off conversations from the first week. If you see cases the agent could have solved alone, your rules are too strict. If you see customers who got frustrated before reaching a person, they are too loose. Adjust one rule at a time so you know which one moved the result.
