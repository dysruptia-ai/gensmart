The conversational sales funnel is the path a person follows from their first message to the purchase, all inside one conversation. It works when each stage has a clear owner. In GenSmart, the agent handles answering, qualifying and booking; your team makes the decisions that need judgment: custom quotes, negotiation, closing and collecting payment.

## What are the stages of the funnel?

Five stages are enough for most businesses. You can give them your own names; what matters is that each one answers a question: what does this person want and what comes next.

1. <strong>First contact.</strong> The person writes with a question. The agent greets them and answers with the information you loaded. Your team keeps that information up to date.
2. <strong>Qualification.</strong> The agent asks what it needs (what for, by when, what quantity) and assigns a score from 0 to 10. Your team decides which score deserves immediate attention.
3. <strong>Appointment or call.</strong> The agent offers time slots and books on the calendar. Your team prepares using the conversation summary.
4. <strong>Proposal.</strong> A person prepares the custom quote, handles objections and negotiates. The agent keeps answering general questions in the meantime.
5. <strong>Closing and payment.</strong> This is a person's decision. The agent does not close sales or collect payment.

## What falls to the agent and what falls to your team?

The agent takes everything repeatable: answering what you already know how to answer, asking for the usual details and leaving the contact record complete. Your team takes what changes case by case. Splitting it this way keeps someone from spending the afternoon explaining opening hours while a serious prospect waits for a reply.

## When does the conversation go to a person?

It goes in two cases: when the agent does not know the answer and when the contact's score is high. In both, your team receives the full conversation and does not have to ask again what the customer already said.

## What happens to contacts that do not move forward?

They stay in their stage, and that is information too. A contact who has gone days without replying at the proposal stage calls for a decision: pick the conversation back up with something new or consider it closed. What does not help is leaving it in limbo, because the funnel fills with names nobody will serve and stops reflecting your reality. A short review at the end of the week is enough.

:::nota
Write the five stages on a sheet and, next to each, a name: who answers. If a stage has no owner, that is where your prospects get stuck.
:::
