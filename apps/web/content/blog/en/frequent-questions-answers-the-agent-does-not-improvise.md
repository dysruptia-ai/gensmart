An agent does not improvise when every frequent question has a written answer that is complete and has its limits. GenSmart answers with the information you load into its knowledge base and, if it does not know something, hands the conversation to a person. That is why the important work is not the technology: it is writing well what your team already answers every day.

## Where do frequent questions come from?

From your real conversations, not from what you imagine people ask. Open your messages from the last few weeks, note each question and group those that mean the same thing in different words. Also ask whoever answers the phone: they usually know which doubts keep repeating.

## How do you write an answer that leaves no room for improvising?

1. <strong>Answer what was asked first.</strong> If the question is "how much does it cost?", the first sentence gives the amount or explains what it depends on.
2. <strong>Include the conditions.</strong> Validity, city, minimums, exclusions. What you do not write, the agent does not know.
3. <strong>Mark what cannot be said.</strong> If something needs a person (a custom quote, an exception), write it that way: "the team decides this".
4. <strong>Use your customer's words.</strong> If people say "delivery" and you write "shipping service", add both.

## What does the agent do with a question that is not written down?

It does not invent the answer: it says it will pass the conversation to a person and leaves it summarized. That question is a valuable clue, because it tells you what is missing from the base.

## How long should an answer be?

As long as it takes to resolve the doubt, not one line more. A good answer has the fact, the condition that goes with it and, if relevant, the next step: "shipping to Medellín takes what your table says, and if you like I can send you the payment link". Long explanations read badly on a phone and people end up asking the same thing again.

## What mistakes make the agent sound like it is improvising?

- Answers too short that leave out the important condition, such as validity or city.
- Contradictory information between two answers, for example two prices for the same thing.
- Text copied from a long document, with phrases nobody would say in a conversation.
- Answers nobody has reviewed since the offer changed.

Almost all of them are fixed by reading aloud. If an answer sounds odd when you say it, it will also sound odd when the customer reads it.

:::nota
Each week, review the conversations that went to a person for lack of an answer. Turn the three or four most repeated into new answers. Before long, the base looks like your actual business.
:::

## Where do you start?

With ten questions, not a hundred. Write them well, test them yourself from your phone with the GenSmart free trial and grow the base as real cases appear.
